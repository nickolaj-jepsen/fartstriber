# Calibrating a car kit

A kit error poisons every design: take the time. Never edit a kit's files by hand; everything goes through `livery kit` and `livery sim` commands. How each step works is in `docs/architecture.md` (Kits, The sim).

## Steps

1. **Template and replay.** The owner downloads the car's paint template PSD and saves a replay of themselves driving the car (lone qualifying, so no traffic covers it). Check the PSD has `Turn Off Before Exporting TGA` with `Mask`, `Wire`, `Number Blocks` and `Sponsor Blocks`, and a `Custom Spec Map` group; read the spec group's channel meanings from its layer names (iRacing: R metallic, G roughness, B clearcoat). Don't guess them. The replay's track needs its camera set saved once from the sim's camera tool (Ctrl+F12); Daytona road already has one.
2. **Add.** Ask the owner to open the replay, then run `livery kit add <psd> --car <kit> --paint-folder <folder>` in the background; the paint folder is the session's `CarPath` (`livery sim status` shows it). It runs every step not yet done and stops with a `next:` line wherever it needs something: do that (or ask the owner to), then run the same command again. Tell the owner each thing it waits for, in one message:
   - camera.ini has to point at the track's set: it waits until they close the sim, writes the cameras, and they start it and open the replay again;
   - the cameras are written: they close and reopen the replay, and the frame pick starts once the sim lists them;
   - the cameras don't fit the car: `pick-frame` sized them for it on new groups, and they reopen the replay once more.
3. **Look at what it made.** The island split (`overlays/islands.png`), keep-clear zones and shut lines (`livery preview flat` of any design on the kit shows them on the texture overlay). The frame pick's run folder (`.cache/pick-frame/<kit>/<run>/`): `report.json` gives each candidate's problems per view, and the white-car shots must show the whole car, centred, with nothing between camera and car. Then `livery sim pick-frame <kit> --check`: every view framed the same in either order.
4. **Capture.** Once the frame is picked, `kit add` shoots the calibration capture (`livery kit capture <kit>`, about 12 minutes; each paint is a sim run of its own, so other runs queue between paints) and reads its summary into the log: every view should decode nearly all of the car. Keep the frames of a doubtful run (`--keep-frames`): pattern and inverse must be complementary, never a plain grey car. Then `livery bake check <kit> [designs]`: it renders the calibration livery (and the designs) from the capture and compares each with one real preview run; until it passes, builders and the critic can't use baked views. Baked views stand in for a design's sim shots only for the finishes the check covers (`finishes covered`: those of the designs that passed), so name a design for each finish designs use; a design that fails covers none of its finishes, so keep metal apart from the gloss fixtures. A later check of other designs adds to what the last one covered. Check again after any change to `livery/bake_render.py`, and shoot the capture again after an iRacing update (`livery bake status` names what moved). Look at each view's background frame for anything that hides the car (a bank, a wall): the fit fills what one side doesn't see from the other, but a view that misses most of a side needs another frame.
5. **Fit.** `kit add` runs `livery kit fit <kit>` from the capture; on a kit that has maps, run `livery kit fit <kit> --compare` first: it writes nothing and gives, island by island, the new map's p90 and the current one's on the same samples, how far paint moves, both fits' `sides_agree` and how much of each approved design moves. Write the new maps when they are at least as good on every main island (`*`); `--keep <ids>` keeps the current maps where they aren't. The written report is `fit-report.json` in the kit: per-island residuals, mirror partners, seam pairs, the frames (the left side matched to the right, `frames`), the top view's u scale (`top.u_p90`, about 0.003) and which strips moved to the sides (`moved_from`). Judge an island by `p90_px` and `dropped` (all its samples), not `rms_px` (only the ones the robust fit kept, which hides a map that can't follow its island to the edge). Large side islands should have `p90_px` under ~2 and small ones under ~8; a large one with `dropped` above ~0.1 misfits where its side sees it edge-on, which its top map takes over. `sides_agree` gives each partner pair's miss, one reflected, at the same (u, v), a few pixels at most; the fit replaces a pair's worse map past that (`replaced_by_mirror_of`) and flags one it can't judge (`disagrees_with`). An island's `top` entry is its up-facing part's top-view map (`map: null`: left unmapped). A new capture fixes a bad capture, not a misfit: look at where the samples miss (by u and v) before shooting again.
6. **Label.** Name the body so designs never hard-code an island id or a measured position: write `parts.json` (each part's islands, side, confidence and evidence; a `cut` polygon where one island holds two parts; groups; `unnamed` islands with what they are) and `features.json` (arches, shut lines and creases, the window line, number zones in car space, and every sim overlay and occluder: what the sim draws over the paint is a `keep_clear` feature, a part of the car that hides paint from some views an `occluder`). The format is `body.yaml`'s sections as JSON (docs/architecture.md, Kits). Find islands from the calibration capture's `geometry.npz` (which texel each screen pixel shows) and the UI view, and check names against mirror partners. Keep what you couldn't confirm, marked `unsure`; put open questions and the map problems you find (a map claiming paint the sim never shows) under `questions` and `fit_problems`. `livery kit label <kit> parts.json features.json`; it refuses ids and features that don't fit the kit. A relabel starts from `body.yaml` as it is. Label after the fit, whose maps the features are checked against; a label changes the kit, so the next `kit add` run (or `livery kit review <kit>`) reviews it again with the labelled pictures.
7. **Finishes.** `kit add` runs `livery kit spec <kit>` after the capture: it writes, from the capture's ladders, the compensation for each preset with metal in it, and its summary says how far each finish reads from gloss in each view (`docs/sim.md`, Rendering). A finish that doesn't read apart from gloss gets a new preset: `livery kit spec <kit> --preset satin=0,110,64` shoots that preset's ladder into the capture (seven paints) and writes the compensation again.
8. **Bleed.** Paint the islands dark on white with 0, 4, 8, 16 px bleed and look for white seam lines in far shots; set `bleed_px` to the smallest width that removes them (16 for the McLaren).
9. **Calibration.** `kit add` ends with `livery kit review <kit>`: overlays (islands by view, and parts and features by name: `parts.png`, `body.png`) plus the calibration livery (bands at fixed car-space places, drawn through this kit's maps) on the kit's car in the sim. Bands must meet across every seam and the beltline. Check at native resolution, not a downscale, first in the side views, whose screen is square to the car: the beltline runs level there and at the same height on both sides; in the top and three-quarter views every band runs on over the fender and haunch tops onto the top without a gap or a jog; and against the body, the same distance below both wheel-arch tops. The owner catches a 4 px step. Fix and repeat; known weak spots go in the review note.
10. **Approval.** `livery review --kit <kit>`; the owner checks the names, the unsure ones and the open questions, and accepts (writes the kit's `approved.json`, which any later change to the kit voids) or asks for edits.
11. **Approved designs.** A refit moves every design on the kit. `livery build --all` in the background, then tell the owner which approved designs it reports as changed, with each one's diff picture; a changed design goes back to `livery review <design>` before it is installed again.
12. **Retro.** End the session with the `retro` skill (`/retro`).
