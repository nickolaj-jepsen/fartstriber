# Calibrating a car kit

A kit error poisons every design: take the time. Never edit a kit's files by hand; everything goes through `livery kit` and `livery sim` commands. How each step works is in `docs/architecture.md` (Kits, The sim).

## Steps

1. **Template and replay.** The owner downloads the car's paint template PSD and saves a replay of themselves driving the car (lone qualifying, so no traffic covers it). Read the PSD's `Custom Spec Map` channel meanings from its layer names (iRacing: R metallic, G roughness, B clearcoat). Don't guess them. The replay's track needs its camera set saved once from the sim's camera tool (Ctrl+F12).
2. **Add.** Ask the owner to open the replay, then run `livery kit add <psd> --car <kit> --paint-folder <folder>` in the background; the paint folder is the session's `CarPath` (`livery sim status` shows it). It stops with a `next:` line wherever it needs something: do that (or ask the owner to), then run the same command again. Tell the owner up front, in one message, every close and reopen of the sim or the replay it can wait for (`livery kit add --help`, and `livery sim pick-frame --help` for cameras that don't fit the car).
3. **Look at what it made.** The island split (`overlays/islands.png`), keep-clear zones and shut lines (`livery preview flat` of any design on the kit shows them on the texture overlay). The frame pick's run folder, which `kit add` prints with the frame: `report.json` gives each candidate's problems per view, and the white-car shots must show the whole car, centred, with nothing between camera and car. Then `livery sim pick-frame <kit> --check`.
4. **Capture.** Once the frame is picked, `kit add` shoots the calibration capture (`livery kit capture <kit>`): every view should decode nearly all of the car. Keep the frames of a doubtful run (`--keep-frames`): pattern and inverse must be complementary, never a plain grey car. Then `livery bake check <kit>`: until it passes, builders and the critic can't use baked views. Look at each view's background frame for anything that hides the car (a bank, a wall): the fit fills what one side doesn't see from the other, but a view that misses most of a side needs another frame.
5. **Fit.** `kit add` runs `livery kit fit <kit>` from the capture; on a kit that has maps, run `livery kit fit <kit> --compare` first, and write the new maps only when they are at least as good on every main island (`*`); `--keep <ids>` keeps the current maps where they aren't. In the kit's `fit-report.json`, judge an island by `p90_px` and `dropped` (all its samples), not `rms_px` (only the ones the robust fit kept, which hides a map that can't follow its island to the edge). Large side islands should have `p90_px` under ~2; a large one with `dropped` above ~0.1 misfits where its side sees it edge-on, which its top map takes over. Look at every island flagged `disagrees_with`: its two sides disagree and the fit couldn't judge which map is right. A new capture fixes a bad capture, not a misfit: look at where the samples miss (by u and v) before shooting again.
6. **Label.** Name the body so designs never hard-code an island id or a measured position: write `parts.json` (every island in a part, or under `unnamed` with what it is) and `features.json` (in car space: arches, shut lines and creases, the window line, number zones, every sim overlay as `keep_clear` and every occluder), as `body.yaml`'s sections in JSON (docs/architecture.md, Kits), then `livery kit label <kit> parts.json features.json`. Find islands from the calibration capture's `geometry.npz` (which texel each screen pixel shows) and the UI view, and check names against mirror partners. Keep what you couldn't confirm, marked `unsure`; put open questions and the map problems you find (a map claiming paint the sim never shows) under `questions` and `fit_problems`. A relabel starts from `body.yaml` as it is. Label after the fit, whose maps the features are checked against; a label changes the kit, so the next `kit add` run (or `livery kit review <kit>`) reviews it again with the labelled pictures.
7. **Finishes.** `kit add` runs `livery kit spec <kit>` after the capture; its summary says how far each finish reads from gloss in each view (`docs/sim.md`, Materials). A finish that doesn't read apart from gloss gets a new preset: `livery kit spec <kit> --preset <material>=R,G,B` shoots that preset's ladder into the capture and writes the compensation again.
8. **Bleed.** Paint the islands dark on white with 0, 4, 8, 16 px bleed and look for white seam lines in far shots; set the smallest width that removes them with `livery kit spec <kit> --bleed <px>`.
9. **Calibration.** `kit add` ends with `livery kit review <kit>`: the labelled overlays plus the calibration livery (bands at fixed car-space places, drawn through this kit's maps) on the kit's car in the sim. Bands must meet across every seam and the beltline. Check at native resolution, not a downscale, first in the side views, whose screen is square to the car: the beltline runs level there and at the same height on both sides; in the top and three-quarter views every band runs on over the fender and haunch tops onto the top without a gap or a jog; and against the body, the same distance below both wheel-arch tops. The owner catches a 4 px step. Fix and repeat; known weak spots go in the review note.
10. **Approval.** `livery review --kit <kit>`; the owner checks the names, the unsure ones and the open questions, and accepts (writes the kit's `approved.json`, which any later change to the kit voids) or asks for edits.
11. **Approved designs.** A refit moves every design on the kit. `livery build --all` in the background, then tell the owner which approved designs it reports as changed, with each one's diff picture; a changed design goes back to `livery review <design>` before it is installed again.
12. **Retro.** End the session with the `retro` skill (`/retro`).
